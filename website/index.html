In the works

The planned end-to-end encryption channel comes in two steps: first files are sealed into an encryption envelope locally, then the transport channel delivers the envelope to the other device — the encryption envelope is implemented; channel integration is in progress. More tools are planned with nothing announceable yet and no dates promised.

The current, honest picture of transport today is in the privacy policy and in “Data & permissions” below.

Data & permissions

Local actions and online transfer are handled separately.

Full privacy & data handling notes →

Local file actions & screenshots

Copy path, quick edit, new file, and other local actions never touch the network. Screenshots need the screen-recording permission and their history stays on your Mac; when you are signed in and auto-sync is on, a screenshot that has been copied is uploaded to the relay. The optional Finder long-press entry additionally needs the accessibility permission.

Online sending & sharing

Relay transfer and storage today do not provide end-to-end encryption — the server can access the files and metadata it stores. The official service uses HTTPS; self-hosted services also need HTTPS when used across networks.

The encryption envelope is implemented; channel integration is in progress. End-to-end encryption is not live yet, so we do not claim “only you can read it”.

Pick the entry point for your device

macOS is fully featured, Windows is an experimental subset, and the phone apps receive — check before downloading.

macOS 13+ (Apple Silicon)

The most complete platform: file radial menu, screenshot pinning, and cross-device transfer. The public preview is ad-hoc signed and not yet notarized with a Developer ID; on first launch right-click → “Open”.

Windows 10+ (x64)

Downloads will be offered here in the future. The basics of the local radial menu: seven core actions plus convert to JPG/PNG/MP4, with a settings page shared with macOS (HEVC→H.264 needs the “HEVC Video Extensions”). This is an experimental build: no screenshots, no signed-in transfer, no AirDrop, and it has not been validated on a real Windows machine; unzip the whole ZIP before running, and SmartScreen may appear on first run.

Android

Receiver client (since v0.1.9): sign in and pair, inbox sync, share-link management, and clipboard-image sending. Sync happens in the foreground with no background push; the APK is debug-signed, installation requires allowing installs from unknown sources — the download will be offered here in the future.

Browser

Nothing to install: sign in and view files and clipboard content in the web inbox — send and receive both ways.

iPhone / iPad: the iOS build signs in, receives, and views content; there is no public install entry yet — the web inbox works in the meantime.

FAQ

Browse the full guides by task

What works without an account? ＋

Local file actions and format conversion, screenshots, saving, pinning, and AirDrop need no relay account. Device transfer and creating share links in the app require signing in; the no-signup web share channel stopped accepting new uploads on 2026-10-20, and in-app sharing is unaffected. The full list of 35+ local actions is on the radial menu page.

Will my clipboard be uploaded automatically? ＋

Plain text is sent only when you choose “Send clipboard to phone”. Screenshots have a separate auto-sync switch, on by default: once signed in, a screenshot is uploaded when it finishes and is copied. You can turn this off in Settings → Screenshot.

The Finder context menu doesn’t appear? ＋

Put Kodeoff into /Applications and launch it once, then enable the Finder extension in System Settings. Inside iCloud Drive and other cloud folders, use Services → “Open Kodeoff radial menu” instead. See the setup and troubleshooting steps.

What system permissions does it need? ＋

Dragging to summon the radial menu needs no accessibility permission; screenshots need screen recording; the left-Shift summon and the Finder long-press entry need accessibility. File access follows your macOS permission settings.

How do I install the Android client? ＋

The Android receiver is not offered for download yet; a future release will offer it on this site. Installation requires allowing installs from unknown sources, and it cannot coexist with a future store-signed build. Sign in to your relay account to send, receive, and sync; the app polls while in the foreground, and messages arrive on next open when it isn’t.

Licensing and open source? ＋

The code is not open source. Licensing and pricing have not been announced.
